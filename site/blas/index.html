Teanga Bench: Irish, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Manx, Breton and Cornish

Teanga Bench measures how well large language models handle six Celtic languages: Irish, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Manx, Breton and Cornish. Every model answers the same few hundred items per test, taken from the Blas course, dictionaries and parallel corpora: lesson exercises, word forms, translation, round-trip translation, and finding and fixing errors as a tutor would. Each item is asked several times. The score is the share of items answered correctly; translation tests give partial credit for closeness to reference translations.

How to read this

  • Score. The share of items the model got right, averaged over repeats. Translation tests give partial credit by chrF++ similarity to reference translations. The overall score weights every test equally.
  • Interval. The bar is the score; the thin line across it is the 95% interval, found by resampling the test items 2,000 times. When two models' intervals overlap, the gap between them may be chance.
  • Sources, not reviewers. Items and answers are taken from the sources named in the methodology. No one has reviewed them by hand, so a few gold answers may be wrong. Tutor explanations are marked by two judge models, not by people.
  • Private items. The full item sets are private so that models cannot be trained on them. 20 items per test are public, and the failure examples on the model pages come only from those.
  • Cost. What one run of this suite cost on OpenRouter at the prices listed on the day it ran, judge models included.

Results by test

Lesson exercises

Multiple-choice, word-order and fill-the-gap questions from the Blas course, marked against the course's accepted answers.

Word forms

Give one inflected form, such as a verb tense, a plural or a prepositional pronoun, marked against a list of accepted forms.

Translation

Translate a sentence into or out of the language, scored by chrF++ similarity to one or more reference translations.

Round trip

Translate a sentence into another language, then back again in a fresh conversation, scored by chrF++ similarity to the original.

Tutor accuracy

Find and fix the error in a sentence, or leave a correct sentence alone; an item counts only when the error is found and fixed and nothing else is changed.
