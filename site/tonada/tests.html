Lesson exercises

#

The task. Answer one closed exercise from the Tonada English course, with the instruction in the learner's first language.

Marking. By rule: the answer must match one of the course's accepted answers.

400 items · 4 first languages · each asked 3 times · 9 models with a complete result

Example item from the public sample en-ch05-u06-recall-descriptions-routines-d2@ja

Prompt

This is an exercise from an English course for Japanese speakers. The learner sees the instruction in Japanese; the answer is in English.

Instruction (Japanese): 日付の前に入る語を選びましょう：“Our meeting is ___ the third of April”.

Options:
- at
- on
- in

Reply with exactly one of the options, copied as written.
Reply with the answer on one line, in this exact form:
ANSWER: <answer>

Accepted answer

on

Tutor accuracy

#

The task. Check an English sentence written by a learner: say whether it has an error, give the wrong words and the correction, and explain in the learner's language.

Marking. By rule: an item counts only when the error is found and fixed and nothing else changes, or a correct sentence is left alone. Two judge models mark the explanation separately.

360 items · 4 first languages · each asked 3 times · 9 models with a complete result

Example item from the public sample en-ch18-c-ja-lesson-s4@ja:err

Prompt

A learner of English whose first language is Japanese wrote the English sentence below. The sentence may contain one error, or it may be correct.

Sentence: I is reading now.

Decide whether the sentence has an error. If it does, give the exact words that are wrong (error_span), the words that should replace them (correction) and the whole corrected sentence (corrected_sentence). If the sentence is correct, set has_error to false, leave error_span and correction empty, and copy the sentence into corrected_sentence. Write the explanation in Japanese, in one or two short sentences saying what is wrong and why, or why the sentence is correct. Every other field stays in English.

Reply with one JSON object and nothing else, in this form:
{"has_error": true|false, "error_span": "...", "correction": "...", "corrected_sentence": "...", "explanation": "..."}

Expected correction

is → am
Corrected sentence: I am reading now.

How this test set is built

What the tests measure

Teanga Bench measures how well large language models tutor English for people whose first language is Portuguese, Spanish, Vietnamese or Japanese. Every model answers the same few hundred items per test, taken from the Tonada course and a public corpus of sentence edits: closed exercises with the instruction in the learner's language, and finding and fixing errors in English sentences as a tutor would. Each item is asked several times. The score is the share of items answered correctly.

  • Lesson exercises. Closed English exercises from the Tonada course, with the instruction in the learner's first language, marked against the accepted answers. 400 items.
  • Tutor accuracy. Find and fix the error in an English sentence, or leave a correct sentence alone; an item counts only when the error is found and fixed and nothing else is changed. 360 items.

Teanga Bench is open source under Apache-2.0. Code, the public samples and every results file are on GitHub.

Gold answers come from the sources named in the methodology; they have not been reviewed by native speakers.
